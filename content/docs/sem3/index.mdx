# Semester III: Theory & Foundations 

Semester III marks a major pivot in the Computer Science curriculum. We are moving past introductory programming and diving deep into the mathematical and theoretical foundations that power modern software. 

This semester is heavily focused on abstraction, algorithmic efficiency, and rigorous proofs. Here is a breakdown of the coursework:

## Core Courses (15 Credits)

- **CSI247: Data Structures**  
  The backbone of efficient software. This course covers the implementation and mathematical analysis of linear structures, trees, graphs, and hashing, alongside rigorous algorithmic complexity (Big-O).
  
- **CSI243: Functional Programming**  
  A complete paradigm shift. Moving away from state mutation to pure functions, immutability, and declarative logic, bridging the gap between software engineering and mathematical mappings.
  
- **CSI213: Discrete Structures III**  
  The advanced mathematical logic underpinning computer science, diving deeper into combinatorics, advanced graph theory, and formal proof techniques.
  
- **MAT221: Calculus I**  
  The study of continuous mathematics, focusing on limits, derivatives, integrals, and their foundational applications in computer science and engineering.

## Elective (3 Credits)

- **MGT202: Management**  
  Broadening the scope beyond pure technical skills to understand organizational behavior, project lifecycles, and business principles.  
  *(Note: This also serves as a mandatory pre-requisite for MGT303 in future semesters).*
