# Course Overview: CSI213 Discrete Structures III

This curriculum provides a comprehensive, formal exposition of the principles of probability and statistical inference, tailored specifically to the syllabus of CSI213 at the University of Botswana. The material bridges combinatorial mathematics, probability theory, and applied statistical modeling.

## Course Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:
1. Apply combinatorial mathematics to calculate probabilities in complex, restricted sample spaces.
2. Utilize axiomatic probability, conditional inference, and Bayes' Theorem to update beliefs based on new evidence.
3. Select, parameterize, and apply appropriate discrete and continuous probability distributions to model real-world stochastic processes.
4. Evaluate the operational integrity of multi-component systems using reliability engineering principles.
5. Perform simple linear regression and correlation analysis to quantify relationships between variables.
6. Apply the Central Limit Theorem and sampling distributions to conduct statistical inference and expected value calculations.

## Syllabus Alignment

The course is structured to fulfill the following core academic objectives:
1. **Combinatorial Analysis:** Permutations, combinations, and counting principles in varied numeral systems.
2. **Probability Theory:** Axiomatic foundations, conditional probability, and Bayesian inference.
3. **Discrete Distributions:** Mathematical formulation and application of Binomial, Poisson, Geometric, Negative Binomial, and Hypergeometric models.
4. **Continuous Distributions:** Exponential and Normal distributions, including standardization and memoryless properties.
5. **Applied Statistics:** System reliability, simple linear regression, and correlation analysis.
6. **Statistical Inference:** Sampling distributions, the Central Limit Theorem, and expected value calculations.

## Assessment Structure

*   **Continuous Assessment (50%):** Comprising five formal quizzes and two major term tests.
*   **Final Examination (50%):** A comprehensive evaluation of all theoretical and applied topics.

## Course Summary

CSI213 transitions students from deterministic mathematical thinking to stochastic reasoning. By mastering the calculus of probability and the mechanics of statistical distributions, students acquire the analytical framework necessary to model uncertainty, evaluate risk, and extract actionable insights from empirical data. This foundation is critical for advanced studies in computer science, data science, and engineering.
