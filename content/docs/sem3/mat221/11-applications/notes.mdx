## Modelling with a slice

An application integral adds infinitely many small geometric contributions. The main difficulty is usually not integration; it is describing one correct slice.

Always sketch first, mark the bounds, identify the axis, and label distances as positive lengths.

## Area between curves

For vertical slices where $f$ is above $g$,

$$
\boxed{A=\int_a^b[f(x)-g(x)]dx}.
$$

The property “top minus bottom” makes every slice height nonnegative. With horizontal slices, use “right minus left” and integrate with respect to $y$.

## Volumes: when to use each method

- Disks/washers use slices perpendicular to the axis of rotation.
- Shells use slices parallel to the axis.

Disk and washer formulas:

$$
V=\pi\int_a^bR^2dx,
\qquad
\boxed{V=\pi\int_a^b(R^2-r^2)dx}.
$$

Here $R$ is the distance from the axis to the farther boundary and $r$ to the nearer boundary.

Shell formula:

$$
\boxed{V=2\pi\int_a^b(\text{radius})(\text{height})dx}.
$$

The shell radius is distance to the axis; shell height is top minus bottom.

## Worked example: washers

The curves $y=2x$ and $y=x^2$ meet where $2x=x^2$, so $x=0$ or $x=2$. On $[0,2]$, $2x$ is above $x^2$.

Rotate the region about the $x$-axis. Vertical slices are perpendicular to the axis and create washers:

$$
R=2x,\qquad r=x^2.
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
V
&=\pi\int_0^2[(2x)^2-(x^2)^2]dx\\
&=\pi\int_0^2(4x^2-x^4)dx\\
&=\pi\left[\frac43x^3-\frac15x^5\right]_0^2\\
&=\pi\left(\frac{32}{3}-\frac{32}{5}\right)\\
&=\boxed{\frac{64\pi}{15}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Worked example: shells

Rotate the same region about the $y$-axis. Vertical slices are parallel to the axis, so shells avoid solving for $x$ in terms of $y$:

$$
\text{radius}=x,\qquad\text{height}=2x-x^2.
$$

Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
V
&=2\pi\int_0^2x(2x-x^2)dx\\
&=2\pi\int_0^2(2x^2-x^3)dx\\
&=2\pi\left[\frac23x^3-\frac14x^4\right]_0^2\\
&=\boxed{\frac{8\pi}{3}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Arc length and surface area

For $y=f(x)$, the differential Pythagorean property is

$$
ds=\sqrt{dx^2+dy^2}=\sqrt{1+(y')^2}\,dx.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{L=\int_a^b\sqrt{1+(y')^2}dx}.
$$

Rotating that small arc about the $x$-axis sweeps circumference $2\pi y$, so

$$
\boxed{S=2\pi\int_a^by\sqrt{1+(y')^2}dx}.
$$

About another axis, replace $y$ with the appropriate distance to that axis.

## Centre of mass and centroid

For a uniform region between $f$ and $g$,

$$
A=\int_a^b(f-g)dx,
$$

$$
\boxed{\bar x=\frac1A\int_a^bx(f-g)dx},
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\bar y=\frac1A\int_a^b\frac12(f^2-g^2)dx}.
$$

The $\bar y$ integrand comes from strip midpoint $\frac12(f+g)$ multiplied by strip area $(f-g)dx$; their product is $\frac12(f^2-g^2)dx$.

## Common errors

- Writing a formula before sketching the region.
- Using $R-r$ instead of $R^2-r^2$.
- Treating a coordinate as a radius without measuring from the axis.
- Mixing $dx$ with functions written in $y$.
- Forgetting the derivative inside arc length.
- Using $(f-g)^2/2$ for $\bar y$ instead of $(f^2-g^2)/2$.
