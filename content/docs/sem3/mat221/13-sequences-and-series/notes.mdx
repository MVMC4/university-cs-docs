## Sequence versus series

A sequence $(a_n)$ is an ordered list. It converges to $L$ when its terms approach $L$.

An infinite series $\sum a_n$ is the limit of its partial sums

$$
S_N=a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_N.
$$

The series converges to $S$ when $S_N\to S$. This distinction is essential: $a_n\to0$ does not guarantee that $\sum a_n$ converges.

## Sequence properties

- A convergent sequence is bounded.
- A bounded monotone sequence converges.
- Limits respect sums, products, and quotients when the denominator limit is nonzero.

For example,

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{\sqrt{n^2+1}}
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1{\sqrt{1+1/n^2}}=1.
$$

## Geometric series

For

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}ar^n,
$$

the partial sum is

$$
S_N=a\frac{1-r^{N+1}}{1-r}.
$$

If $|r|<1$, then $r^{N+1}\to0$, giving

$$
\boxed{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}ar^n=\frac{a}{1-r}}.
$$

If $|r|\ge1$, the series diverges.

## When to use each convergence test

1. Divergence test first: if $a_n\not\to0$, the series diverges.
2. Geometric test when consecutive terms have a constant ratio.
3. $p$-series test for $\sum1/n^p$, which converges iff $p>1$.
4. Ratio test for factorials and exponentials in $n$.
5. Root test when the entire term is raised to the $n$th power.
6. Comparison for positive rational or radical terms.
7. Integral test for positive, continuous, decreasing terms.
8. Alternating-series test for alternating signs.

## Worked example: ratio test

Determine whether $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n!/3^n$ converges. Let $a_n=n!/3^n$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
L
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(n+1)!}{3^{n+1}}\frac{3^n}{n!}\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n+1}{3}=\infty.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $L>1$, the series diverges.

## Alternating, absolute, and conditional convergence

For $\sum(-1)^nb_n$, the alternating-series test requires

$$
b_n\ge0,\qquad b_{n+1}\le b_n\text{ eventually},\qquad b_n\to0.
$$

If $\sum|a_n|$ converges, then $\sum a_n$ converges absolutely. If $\sum a_n$ converges but $\sum|a_n|$ diverges, convergence is conditional.

For the alternating harmonic series,

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}n,
$$

$1/n$ decreases to zero, so the series converges. But $\sum1/n$ diverges, so it converges conditionally.

## Common errors

- Claiming convergence because $a_n\to0$.
- Using the ratio test when $L=1$ and still giving a verdict.
- Checking alternation but not decrease and limit zero.
- Confusing a sequence limit with a series sum.
- Using comparison inequalities in the wrong direction.
