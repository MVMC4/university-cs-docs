## Form and centre

A power series centred at $a$ is

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(x-a)^n.
$$

It is an infinite polynomial whose convergence depends on $x$. There is a radius $R$ such that the series converges for $|x-a|<R$ and diverges for $|x-a|>R$.

## Why and when they are useful

Power series represent functions using polynomials. Inside the interval of convergence, they can be differentiated and integrated term by term, which creates new representations and approximations.

## Complete radius-and-interval method

1. Identify the centre $a$.
2. Apply the ratio or root test to the absolute value of the general term.
3. Solve the inequality $L<1$ to obtain $|x-a|<R$.
4. Convert it to an open interval.
5. Substitute each endpoint into the original series.
6. Test the two numerical endpoint series separately.
7. State both $R$ and the interval of convergence.

## Fully worked example

Find the interval of convergence of

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^n}{n}.
$$

Let $a_n=x^n/n$. The ratio test gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
L
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{x^{n+1}/(n+1)}{x^n/n}\right|\\
&=|x|\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{n+1}\\
&=|x|.
\end{aligned}
$$

Convergence requires $|x|<1$, so $R=1$ and the provisional interval is $(-1,1)$.

At $x=1$:

$$
\sum\frac1n
$$

is harmonic and diverges.

At $x=-1$:

$$
\sum\frac{(-1)^n}{n}
$$

converges by the alternating-series test. Therefore

$$
\boxed{R=1,\qquad\text{interval }[-1,1)}.
$$

## Term-by-term calculus

Inside $|x-a|<R$,

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(x-a)^n
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nc_n(x-a)^{n-1},
$$

and

$$
\int\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(x-a)^n dx
=C+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_n}{n+1}(x-a)^{n+1}.
$$

The radius remains the same, but endpoint behaviour may change and must be retested.

Starting from the geometric property

$$
\frac1{1-x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^n,\qquad |x|<1,
$$

differentiate both sides:

$$
\boxed{\frac1{(1-x)^2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nx^{n-1}},\qquad |x|<1.
$$

## Common errors

- Reporting only $R$ and omitting endpoints.
- Testing endpoints with the ratio test, which is inconclusive there.
- Forgetting the centre $a$ in $|x-a|<R$.
- Assuming differentiation preserves endpoint inclusion.
- Using a derived series outside its interval of convergence.
