## The substitution

Let

$$
t=\tan\frac{x}{2}.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{\sin x=\frac{2t}{1+t^2},\qquad
\cos x=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\qquad
dx=\frac{2\,dt}{1+t^2}}.
$$

These three replacements turn the trigonometric integrand into a rational function of $t$. Then factor, complete the square, or use partial fractions.

## Worked example

Evaluate

$$
\int\frac{dx}{1+\sin x}.
$$

Substitute $\sin x=2t/(1+t^2)$ and $dx=2dt/(1+t^2)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\frac{dx}{1+\sin x}
&=\int\frac{\frac{2\,dt}{1+t^2}}{1+\frac{2t}{1+t^2}}\\
&=\int\frac{2\,dt}{t^2+2t+1}\\
&=2\int(t+1)^{-2}\,dt\\
&=-\frac2{t+1}+C.
\end{aligned}
$$

Return to $x$:

$$
\boxed{-\frac{2}{1+\tan(x/2)}+C}.
$$

Different-looking antiderivatives may be equivalent up to a constant. Differentiate your result to verify it.

## Second worked example: simplify to an arctangent

Evaluate

$$
\int\frac{dx}{2+\cos x}.
$$

Using

$$
\cos x=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\qquad dx=\frac{2dt}{1+t^2},
$$

gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\frac{dx}{2+\cos x}
&=\int\frac{2dt/(1+t^2)}{2+(1-t^2)/(1+t^2)}\\
&=\int\frac{2dt}{2(1+t^2)+1-t^2}\\
&=\int\frac{2dt}{t^2+3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

State the standard property

$$
\int\frac{dt}{t^2+a^2}=\frac1a\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{t}{a}\right)+C.
$$

With $a=\sqrt3$,

$$
\boxed{\int\frac{dx}{2+\cos x}
=\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tan(x/2)}{\sqrt3}\right)+C}.
$$

## When to use it

Use Weierstrass when the integrand is rational in sine and cosine and simpler parity, Pythagorean, or product-to-sum identities do not give a short route.

<Callout label="Common error" tone="trap">The factor $2$ in $dx=2dt/(1+t^2)$ comes from differentiating $x/2$. Losing it changes the entire answer.</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- Replacing sine and cosine but leaving $dx$ unchanged.
- Cancelling factors before expressing the entire integrand in $t$.
- Using the universal substitution when a one-line ordinary substitution is clearer.
- Forgetting to replace $t$ by $\tan(x/2)$ in an indefinite answer.
