## Sources of impropriety

An integral is improper when its interval is infinite or its integrand becomes unbounded. The integral is defined by a limit; the infinity symbol is never substituted like a number.

## Recognition checklist

Use a limit for:

- an infinite endpoint, such as $\int_1^\infty f(x)dx$;
- a vertical asymptote at an endpoint;
- a vertical asymptote inside the interval;
- a doubly infinite interval.

The integral converges only when every required limit is finite.

## Definitions and splitting properties

$$
\int_a^\infty f(x)dx=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_a^t f(x)dx.
$$

If $f$ blows up at $c\in(a,b)$, split it:

$$
\int_a^bf(x)dx
=\lim_{t\to c^-}\int_a^t f(x)dx
+\lim_{s\to c^+}\int_s^b f(x)dx.
$$

Both limits must converge independently. Opposite infinities do not cancel.

## The benchmark p-integrals

$$
\boxed{\int_1^\infty\frac{dx}{x^p}\text{ converges iff }p>1}
$$

but

$$
\boxed{\int_0^1\frac{dx}{x^p}\text{ converges iff }p<1}.
$$

The conditions differ because the first tests decay at infinity, while the second tests the strength of a blow-up at zero.

## Worked example 1: infinite endpoint

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_1^\infty\frac{dx}{x^2}
&=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_1^tx^{-2}dx\\
&=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left[-\frac1x\right]_1^t\\
&=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(1-\frac1t\right)\\
&=\boxed{1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The interval has infinite width, but the function decays fast enough to give finite area.

## Worked example 2: interior singularity

Consider $\int_0^2dx/(x-1)$. The integrand blows up at $x=1$, so split before integrating:

$$
\int_0^2\frac{dx}{x-1}
=\int_0^1\frac{dx}{x-1}+\int_1^2\frac{dx}{x-1}.
$$

The left side is

$$
\lim_{t\to1^-}[\ln|x-1|]_0^t
=\lim_{t\to1^-}\ln|t-1|=-\infty.
$$

Therefore the original improper integral diverges. A symmetric calculation giving zero would be a principal value, not ordinary convergence.

## Comparison property

For nonnegative functions:

- if $0\le f\le g$ and $\int g$ converges, then $\int f$ converges;
- if $0\le g\le f$ and $\int g$ diverges, then $\int f$ diverges.

The reverse directions give no conclusion.

## Common errors

- Writing $F(\infty)$ instead of a limit.
- Failing to scan the interval for singularities.
- Not splitting at every singularity.
- Cancelling two divergent pieces.
- Reversing the two p-test conditions.
