## Where the formula comes from

Start with the product rule:

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(uv)=u\frac{dv}{dx}+v\frac{du}{dx}.
$$

Integrate both sides and rearrange:

$$
\boxed{\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du}.
$$

The definite version keeps the boundary term:

$$
\boxed{\int_a^b u\,dv=[uv]_a^b-\int_a^b v\,du}.
$$

<Callout label="The real goal">The formula only helps if the new integral $\int v\,du$ is easier than the original. Choosing symbols is not the goal; creating a simpler integral is.</Callout>

## How to choose $u$ and $dv$

Use LIATE as a guide for $u$: logarithmic, inverse trigonometric, algebraic, trigonometric, exponential. Then let $dv$ be everything left, including $dx$.

Before committing, ask: does differentiating $u$ simplify it, and can I integrate $dv$ immediately?

The properties used are the product rule, linearity of integration, ordinary antiderivative rules, and—when bounds are present—the evaluation property $[uv]_a^b=u(b)v(b)-u(a)v(a)$.

## Worked example 1: one application

Evaluate $\int xe^x\,dx$. Choose

$$
u=x,\quad dv=e^x\,dx,\quad du=dx,\quad v=e^x.
$$

Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int xe^x\,dx
&=xe^x-\int e^x\,dx\\
&=xe^x-e^x+C\\
&=\boxed{e^x(x-1)+C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Differentiate $e^x(x-1)$: the product rule gives $e^x(x-1)+e^x=xe^x$, so the answer checks.

## Worked example 2: an invisible product

Evaluate $\int \ln x\,dx$ for $x>0$. Write the integrand as $(\ln x)(1)$:

$$
u=\ln x,\quad dv=dx,\quad du=\frac{dx}{x},\quad v=x.
$$

Therefore

$$
\int\ln x\,dx=x\ln x-\int1\,dx=\boxed{x\ln x-x+C}.
$$

This is why logarithms sit first in LIATE: differentiating $\ln x$ turns it into the simpler $1/x$.

## Worked example 3: definite bounds

Evaluate $\int_0^1 x\tan^{-1}x\,dx$. Choose

$$
u=\tan^{-1}x,\quad dv=x\,dx,\quad du=\frac{dx}{1+x^2},\quad v=\frac{x^2}{2}.
$$

Then

$$
\int_0^1x\tan^{-1}x\,dx
=\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\tan^{-1}x\right]_0^1
-\frac12\int_0^1\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}\,dx.
$$

Since $x^2/(1+x^2)=1-1/(1+x^2)$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1x\tan^{-1}x\,dx
&=\frac{\pi}{8}-\frac12[x-\tan^{-1}x]_0^1\\
&=\boxed{\frac{\pi-2}{4}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<Callout label="Common error" tone="trap">In a definite integral the boundary term is $[uv]_a^b$. Evaluate it at both bounds.</Callout>

## Repeated and cyclic cases

If $u$ is a polynomial, repeat parts until the polynomial differentiates to zero. The tabular method records these repeated steps compactly.

If the original integral reappears, name it $I$, perform parts until $I$ returns, then collect the $I$-terms. For example,

$$
\boxed{\int e^x\sin x\,dx=\frac{e^x}{2}(\sin x-\cos x)+C}.
$$

## Method checklist

1. Identify the product, including any invisible factor $1$.
2. Choose $u$ so differentiation simplifies it.
3. Choose an immediately integrable $dv$.
4. Write $du$ and $v$ before applying the rule.
5. Decide whether to stop, repeat, or solve a cycle.
6. Differentiate the result to check.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing $u$ so that differentiation makes the remaining integral harder.
- Forgetting the minus sign in $uv-\int v\,du$.
- Dropping the boundary evaluation in a definite integral.
- Repeating parts in a cyclic integral without isolating the original integral.
