## Partitioning the interval

Numerical integration estimates a definite integral from function values. Split $[a,b]$ into $n$ equal subintervals:

Split $[a,b]$ into $n$ equal subintervals:

$$
\Delta x=\frac{b-a}{n},\qquad x_i=a+i\Delta x.
$$

## Properties and formulas

The trapezoidal rule joins adjacent points with straight lines:

$$
\boxed{T_n=\frac{\Delta x}{2}[f_0+2f_1+2f_2+\cdots+2f_{n-1}+f_n]}.
$$

Its weights are $1,2,2,\ldots,2,1$.

Simpson's rule fits parabolas across pairs of subintervals:

$$
\boxed{S_n=\frac{\Delta x}{3}[f_0+4f_1+2f_2+4f_3+\cdots+4f_{n-1}+f_n]}.
$$

Its weights are $1,4,2,4,\ldots,2,4,1$, and $n$ must be even.

## How to calculate an approximation

1. Calculate $\Delta x$.
2. List all $n+1$ nodes.
3. Calculate and label every $f_i=f(x_i)$.
4. Write the required weight pattern separately.
5. Form the weighted sum.
6. Multiply by $\Delta x/2$ or $\Delta x/3$.
7. Round only at the end.

## Fully worked example

Approximate $\int_1^3dx/x$ with $n=4$. First,

$$
\Delta x=\frac{3-1}{4}=\frac12.
$$

The nodes and values are

| $i$ | $x_i$ | $f_i=1/x_i$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | $1$ | $1$ |
| 1 | $3/2$ | $2/3$ |
| 2 | $2$ | $1/2$ |
| 3 | $5/2$ | $2/5$ |
| 4 | $3$ | $1/3$ |

For trapezoidal weights $1,2,2,2,1$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_4
&=\frac{1/2}{2}\left[1+2\left(\frac23\right)+2\left(\frac12\right)+2\left(\frac25\right)+\frac13\right]\\
&=\frac14\left(\frac{67}{15}\right)\\
&=\boxed{\frac{67}{60}\approx1.1167}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For Simpson weights $1,4,2,4,1$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_4
&=\frac{1/2}{3}\left[1+4\left(\frac23\right)+2\left(\frac12\right)+4\left(\frac25\right)+\frac13\right]\\
&=\boxed{1.1000}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The exact value is $\ln3\approx1.0986$, so Simpson is closer here.

## Error and shape

If $|f''(x)|\le K$ on $[a,b]$,

$$
|E_T|\le\frac{K(b-a)^3}{12n^2}.
$$

If $|f^{(4)}(x)|\le K$ and $n$ is even,

$$
|E_S|\le\frac{K(b-a)^5}{180n^4}.
$$

Concavity explains the trapezoidal direction: chords lie above a concave-up graph, so trapezoids overestimate; they lie below a concave-down graph, so trapezoids underestimate.

### Worked error-bound example

For $f(x)=1/x$ on $[1,3]$,

$$
f''(x)=\frac{2}{x^3}.
$$

This is positive and decreasing, so its largest value on the whole interval occurs at $x=1$:

$$
K=\max_{[1,3]}|f''(x)|=2.
$$

For $n=4$,

$$
|E_T|\le\frac{2(3-1)^3}{12(4)^2}
=\frac{16}{192}=\boxed{\frac1{12}}.
$$

The actual trapezoidal error is smaller than this bound, as expected; an error bound is a guaranteed ceiling, not a prediction of the exact error.

## Common errors

- Using $n$ nodes instead of $n+1$.
- Giving endpoints weight $2$ or $4$ instead of $1$.
- Using odd $n$ in Simpson's rule.
- Alternating Simpson weights incorrectly.
- Rounding every function value too early.
- Bounding the derivative at one point instead of the whole interval.
