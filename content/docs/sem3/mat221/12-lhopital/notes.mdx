## Indeterminate forms

A form is indeterminate when it does not determine a unique limit. The direct-substitution forms $0/0$ and $\infty/\infty$ may produce many different answers depending on relative rates of change.

## When L'Hopital applies

If direct substitution gives exactly $0/0$ or $\pm\infty/\pm\infty$, and the derivative conditions hold near the limit point, then

$$
\boxed{\lim\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=\lim\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}}
$$

provided the derivative ratio has a limit or diverges appropriately.

Differentiate the numerator and denominator separately. Do not use the quotient rule.

## Why the form must be checked first

The form $1/0$ is not indeterminate: it normally signals an infinite or one-sided limit. Applying L'Hopital to a determinate form can change a correct conclusion into a false one.

## Complete algorithm

1. Substitute or analyse the original expression.
2. State the resulting form.
3. If it is $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$, apply L'Hopital.
4. Simplify before differentiating again.
5. Recheck the new form.
6. Repeat only while the form remains indeterminate.

## Worked example 1: repeated use

Evaluate

$$
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{e^x-1-x}{x^2}.
$$

Direct substitution gives $0/0$, so

$$
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{e^x-1}{2x}.
$$

Substitution still gives $0/0$, so apply the rule again:

$$
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{e^x}{2}=\boxed{\frac12}.
$$

## Rewriting other indeterminate forms

L'Hopital does not apply directly to $0\cdot\infty$, $\infty-\infty$, or indeterminate powers.

- Rewrite $0\cdot\infty$ as a quotient.
- Combine $\infty-\infty$ into one fraction or rationalise.
- For $0^0$, $1^\infty$, or $\infty^0$, take logarithms first.

### Worked example 2: product form

Evaluate $\lim_{x\to0^+}x\ln x$. The direct form is $0\cdot(-\infty)$, so rewrite:

$$
x\ln x=\frac{\ln x}{1/x}.
$$

This is $-\infty/\infty$, so

$$
\lim_{x\to0^+}\frac{1/x}{-1/x^2}
=\lim_{x\to0^+}(-x)=\boxed{0}.
$$

### Worked example 3: power form

Let

$$
L=\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(1+\frac1x\right)^x.
$$

The form is $1^\infty$. Take logarithms:

$$
\ln L=\lim_{x\to\infty}x\ln\left(1+\frac1x\right)
=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\ln(1+1/x)}{1/x}.
$$

This is $0/0$. Differentiate top and bottom:

$$
\ln L=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{-1/[x^2(1+1/x)]}{-1/x^2}
=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac1{1+1/x}=1.
$$

Exponentiate both sides: $L=e^1=\boxed e$.

## Common errors

- Applying the rule without stating the indeterminate form.
- Using the quotient rule.
- Applying it directly to a product, difference, or power form.
- Stopping when the result is still indeterminate.
- Forgetting to exponentiate after solving a logarithmic limit.
