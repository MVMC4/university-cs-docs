<ExamQuestion title="Question 1: Family business stages and systems [11 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Define a family business and name its three structural stages with the generation of each. [5]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>The people who established or acquired the business, or their descendants, significantly influence its strategic decisions and life course [2]</li><li>Owner-managed business (first generation), sibling partnership (second generation) and cousin consortium (third and later generations) [3]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Explain why conflict arises when family principles are applied to business operations. [3]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>The family system is built on care, nurturing, security and loyalty whatever a member contributes [1]</li><li>The business system is driven by efficiency, profitability and rewards for performance [1]</li><li>Applying nurturing to decisions that need strict accountability causes friction [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Name the three circles of the Three-Circle Model and describe sector 7. [3]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Family, ownership and management [1]</li><li>Sector 7 holds family owner-employees: family members who hold shares, sit on the board and work as managers [2]</li></ul></>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Question 2: Socio-emotional wealth and commitment [13 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Define socio-emotional wealth and state the five dimensions of the FIBER model. [6]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>The non-financial value and emotional returns that family members get from owning and running the firm [1]</li><li>Family control and influence [1]</li><li>Identification of the family [1]</li><li>Binding social ties [1]</li><li>Emotional attachment [1]</li><li>Renewal of family bonds [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Distinguish the four commitment profiles of next-generation members and say which performs best. [5]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Desire-based (want to): real passion, giving the best performance and initiative [2]</li><li>Obligation-based (ought to): family duty, with a risk of burnout [1]</li><li>Cost-based (have to): fear of financial loss, giving passive compliance [1]</li><li>Need-based (need to): no alternatives, giving low engagement and conflict [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Give two fears that can stop a successor committing to the firm. [2]</>}
    solution={<>Any two of: fear of failure; fear of success; fear of disappointing parents; fear of disappointing others or outside stakeholders; and, in the textbook list, fear of commitment. [2]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Question 4: Succession and the Iaccarino & Son case [12 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>List five responsibilities of the senior generation in succession planning. [5]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Communicate openly about retirement timing [1]</li><li>Plan proactively and in writing [1]</li><li>Enforce accountability by objective standards [1]</li><li>Develop owners in finance and governance [1]</li><li>Design a long-term retirement exit [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Describe three alternatives when family succession is not viable. [3]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>A professional non-family general manager, with the family keeping board ownership [1]</li><li>Interim management to prepare younger heirs until they are ready [1]</li><li>An outright sale to realise the value of the business for retirement [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Using the Iaccarino & Son case, identify two lessons about succession in a family business. [4]</>}
    solution={<>Any two lessons, each supported by a fact from the case, for two marks each. For example: the founder would not let go of power, and his son had joined without negotiating an ownership position; long-serving employees resisted the next generation's changes; the firm could not shrink fast enough once the recession reached it, and had cut margins to win work. [4]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>
