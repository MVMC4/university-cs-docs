<ExamQuestion title="Question 1: Small business and entrepreneurial opportunity [10 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Define a small business. [3]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>A business that is relatively small compared with the largest firms in its industry [1]</li><li>Operations are often geographically localised and financing comes from a limited number of individuals [1]</li><li>It may begin with one person but can grow beyond a one-person operation [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Define an entrepreneurial opportunity and explain how it differs from a small business. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>An economically attractive and timely opportunity that creates value for customers and owners [2]</li><li>A small business describes the nature and size of the firm, whereas an opportunity describes the attractive, value-creating chance the firm pursues [2]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>A small takeaway sells chicken and chips. Describe how it could become an entrepreneurial opportunity. [3]</>}
    solution={<>It is a small business because of its size and local operation. It becomes an opportunity if it pursues an attractive, timely need and creates value, for example a convenient, affordable meal service designed around the needs of UB students. The key question is whether it is merely small or pursuing an attractive opportunity. [3]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Question 2: From problem to opportunity [10 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>List, in order, the six steps from problem to opportunity. [6]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Identify a problem or unmet need [1]</li><li>Identify who experiences the problem [1]</li><li>Develop a solution [1]</li><li>Determine whether customers value the solution [1]</li><li>Determine whether they are willing to pay [1]</li><li>Build a business model that can create value sustainably [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Why should an entrepreneur start with a customer problem rather than a product? [2]</>}
    solution={<>Value comes from solving a problem someone has and will pay to solve. Starting with a product risks building something no identified customer values. The class activity asks for a specific problem first, and then the customer, the solution and the value. [2]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>State the four things the exit question asks you to name. [2]</>}
    solution={<>Problem, customer, solution and value. [2]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>
