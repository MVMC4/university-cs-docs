<ExamQuestion title="Question 1: Integrity and ethics [10 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Define integrity and explain why it is important to the success of a small business. [5]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>A general sense of honesty and reliability, shown through a strong commitment to doing what is right regardless of the circumstances [2]</li><li>Values, beliefs, words, decisions and actions are consistent [1]</li><li>Importance: it builds trust, which supports repeat business, relationships, loyalty, reputation and long-term sustainability [2]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Distinguish between ethics and integrity, using an example. [3]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Ethics gives principles and standards for what is right and wrong [1]</li><li>Integrity is consistently acting on those principles [1]</li><li>An example such as an owner who knows falsifying records is wrong but does it under financial pressure [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Why is integrity said to be tested under pressure? [2]</>}
    solution={<>It is easy to behave ethically when everyone is watching, there is little to gain and the right choice is obvious. The real test comes when nobody is supervising, there is a chance of personal gain, the business is under financial pressure, others seem to be dishonest, or the person believes they will not be caught. The question is not only "Will I be caught?" but "Is this the right thing to do?" [2]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Question 2: Pressure and the cash sale [10 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Explain how pressure, opportunity and justification can contribute to unethical behaviour. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Pressure: sales targets, profit, debts, keeping a job or the business alive [1]</li><li>Opportunity: weak controls, little supervision, poor accountability [1]</li><li>Justification: "everyone does it", "only this once", "the business needs to survive" [1]</li><li>Together they lead to the unethical action [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>A business receives P10,000 in cash and records only P6,000. State the amount kept outside the records and describe three possible consequences. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>P4,000 is kept outside the official records [1]</li><li>Any three of: inaccurate financial information, poor management decisions, weak internal controls, loss of trust, compliance problems [3]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Describe the chain reaction one unethical decision can create. [2]</>}
    solution={<>Inaccurate information, then wrong management decisions, then loss of trust, then customers or other stakeholders are affected, then reputation is damaged, and the business eventually pays a price. [2]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Question 3: Stakeholders and responsibilities [12 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Identify the major stakeholders of a small business and state what two of them expect. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Owners, customers, employees, suppliers, government and communities [2]</li><li>Two expectations described, for example customers expect accurate information and promised quality, suppliers expect honest communication and reliable payment [2]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Explain the four responsibilities of a small business. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Economic: be financially viable [1]</li><li>Legal: obey laws and regulations, a minimum standard [1]</li><li>Ethical: avoid dishonest, unfair or harmful practices [1]</li><li>Discretionary or social: voluntary contributions to society [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Give two examples of stakeholder interests conflicting and say what the entrepreneur should do. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Two examples, such as lower prices that help customers but cut margins, or higher wages that help employees but raise costs [2]</li><li>Recognise the competing interests, understand the consequences, consider fairness and make responsible trade-offs [2]</li></ul></>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>
