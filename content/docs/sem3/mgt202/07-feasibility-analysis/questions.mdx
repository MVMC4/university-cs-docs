<ExamQuestion title="Question 1: Feasibility analysis and its four areas [14 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Define feasibility analysis and explain four reasons for doing it before major investment. [6]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>A preliminary investigation of whether a business idea is practical and potentially viable, and of the conditions under which it could work [2]</li><li>It tests assumptions [1]</li><li>It finds resource gaps [1]</li><li>It improves the use of money and time, including stopping an unsuitable idea [1]</li><li>It improves later planning [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Name the four areas of feasibility and the main issues in each. [8]</>}

<ExamQuestion title="Question 2: Product or service, industry and market [13 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Distinguish desirability from demand and give one suitable research method for each. [6]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Desirability is the appeal and usefulness of the offering to intended customers [2]</li><li>A concept test or customer interviews can examine it [1]</li><li>Demand is the willingness and ability to buy under stated conditions of price and frequency [2]</li><li>A paid pilot (or a buying-intentions survey, with its limits) can investigate it [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Why does a buying-intentions survey give limited evidence of future sales? [3]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Respondents may overstate what they will actually do [1]</li><li>They may be polite, unable to pay or unrepresentative [1]</li><li>Stated intention is not a purchase, and a small sample cannot give a purchase rate [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Distinguish an industry from a target market, using a Botswana example. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>An industry is the group of businesses offering similar or related goods and services [1]</li><li>A target market is the particular group the venture chooses to serve [1]</li><li>A clear Botswana example of each, such as the laundry and cleaning sector and students in selected accommodation near UB [2]</li></ul></>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Question 3: Organizational feasibility [10 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Identify the two primary issues in organizational feasibility and explain how they differ. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Management prowess: the practical ability of the founder or team to launch and manage the venture [2]</li><li>Resource sufficiency: access to the essential nonfinancial resources needed to deliver the offer [2]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Explain why a founder with strong management skills can still fail a resource sufficiency assessment. [3]</>}
    solution={<>Management skills concern the people, while resource sufficiency concerns access to resources such as premises, dependable transport or a partner with enough capacity. Skills cannot replace an essential resource that is unavailable, or one that rests only on an unconfirmed promise. [3]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>A partner can reliably handle 70 orders a month, but the plan needs 100. Explain why this matters. [3]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Capacity is the volume the business can handle at its promised standard [1]</li><li>The plan needs more than the confirmed 70, so it is not supported until more capacity is secured [1]</li><li>Options include a backup partner, a smaller plan or documented extra capacity, and peak periods must be allowed for [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Question 4: Financial feasibility calculations [16 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>The laundry case has a price of P60, a variable cost of P40 and fixed costs of P2,000 a month. Calculate the contribution per order, the break-even volume and the operating result at 80 orders. [7]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Contribution: P60 - P40 = P20 [1]</li><li>Break-even volume: P2,000 divided by P20 [1]</li><li>Break-even is 100 orders a month [1]</li><li>Contribution at 80 orders: 80 x P20 = P1,600 [2]</li><li>Operating result: P1,600 - P2,000 = a P400 loss [2]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>The variable cost rises to P45 with the price at P60 and fixed costs at P2,000. Calculate the new break-even volume and interpret it. [5]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Contribution falls to P60 - P45 = P15 [1]</li><li>P2,000 divided by P15 = 133.33 [1]</li><li>Round up to 134 whole orders [1]</li><li>The venture must sell more orders to cover the same fixed costs [1]</li><li>It must check whether demand and capacity can support that volume [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Explain how a venture can report an operating surplus while facing a cash shortage. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Profit is revenue less expenses, while cash flow records when money is received and paid [1]</li><li>Credit sales create revenue before the cash arrives [1]</li><li>Suppliers and operating costs must be paid in the meantime [1]</li><li>For example, 120 orders give revenue of P7,200 and a P400 surplus, but if only P3,600 is collected against P6,800 of payments, cash falls by P3,200 [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>
