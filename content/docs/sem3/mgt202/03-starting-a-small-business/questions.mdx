<ExamQuestion title="Question 1: Problem, segment and evidence [12 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Explain why the activity tells groups not to begin with a product. [3]</>}
    solution={<>A good idea rests on a real problem that a particular customer group has. Starting from a product risks building something that nobody has the problem for. The activity asks groups to define the problem first, tell it apart from a proposed solution, and leave the solution until later. [3]</>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Define a minimum viable segment and list the five parameters used to profile it. [5]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>The smallest group of customers who share similar characteristics and experience the same problem [2]</li><li>Location, occupation or role, relevant behaviour, the situation in which the problem occurs, and the common need or difficulty [3]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Describe how a group should gather evidence about its problem. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Speak to at least three potential customers from the selected segment [1]</li><li>Ask about the difficulty, when and where it happens, how often, the consequences, how they manage it now, and what is unsatisfactory [2]</li><li>Record the answers without trying to persuade respondents to accept the preferred solution [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Question 2: The 4U test and the problem statement [11 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Name the four Us and state the question behind each of the first three. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>The four Us: Unworkable, Unavoidable, Urgent and Underserved [1]</li><li>Unworkable: does the problem make it hard to complete an important activity? [1]</li><li>Unavoidable: is it difficult to ignore or avoid? [1]</li><li>Urgent: do customers need it addressed soon? [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Write the formula for the customer problem statement. [3]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>A specific customer group experiences a specific problem [1]</li><li>when a situation or context [1]</li><li>resulting in a measurable or observable consequence [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>Rewrite this as a proper problem statement: "We should build an app so that food vendors can predict how much to cook." State what is wrong with the original. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>The original is a solution, not a problem, and it names no customer group, situation or consequence [2]</li><li>A revised statement such as: small food vendors near offices in Gaborone struggle to estimate daily demand, resulting in food waste on some days and lost sales on others [2]</li></ul></>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Question 3: BLAC, white space and boundaries [11 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Describe the four BLAC categories. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Blatant: already recognised by customers [1]</li><li>Latent: not yet fully recognised [1]</li><li>Aspirational: desirable but not essential [1]</li><li>Critical: critical enough for customers to seek a solution [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>What is the white space, and how much of it should a group develop in this activity? [3]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>The open, underserved area for unique positioning: what is missing from current alternatives, why the problem remains inadequately addressed, why solving it may be an opportunity [2]</li><li>Only briefly; detailed competitive strategy is not developed at this stage [1]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>List four things the activity says a group should not yet do. [4]</>}
    solution={<>Any four of: develop the complete product or service; prepare a Business Model Canvas; design an MVP or prototype; conduct detailed internal and external analysis; select a competitive strategy; prepare financial forecasts; conclude that the idea is feasible. [4]</>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>

<ExamQuestion title="Question 4: Textbook ideas for Chapter 3 [10 marks]">
  <SubQuestion
    part="a"
    prompt={<>Distinguish outside-in analysis from inside-out analysis. [4]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Outside-in studies the context of the venture: the general, industry and competitive environments [2]</li><li>Inside-out assesses the business's own competitive potential through its resources, capabilities and core competencies [2]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="b"
    prompt={<>Name the three broad strategy options and describe one risk of a focus strategy. [3]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>Cost-based, differentiation-based and focus strategies [1]</li><li>Focus markets can erode if competitors imitate the strategy, the segment disappears or loses its uniqueness, or new businesses subdivide the industry [2]</li></ul></>}
  />
  <SubQuestion
    part="c"
    prompt={<>What is a fatal flaw? Give two examples. [3]</>}
    solution={<><ul><li>A circumstance or development that alone could make a new business unsuccessful [1]</li><li>Two examples such as limited market potential, powerful competitors, a weak competitive advantage or high start-up costs [2]</li></ul></>}
  />
</ExamQuestion>
