# CSI131: Discrete Structures I

Discrete mathematics forms the foundational language of computer science. This course transitions students from computational mathematics to formal mathematical reasoning, focusing on the discrete structures that model computational processes, data organization, and algorithmic logic. The primary objective is to develop the analytical maturity required to read, understand, and construct rigorous mathematical proofs.

## Scope and Coverage

The curriculum is designed to build a comprehensive toolkit for theoretical computer science. Core topics include:

1. **Propositional and Predicate Logic**: Truth tables, logical equivalences, De Morgan's Laws, quantifiers, and formal rules of inference.
2. **Methods of Proof**: Direct proofs, proof by contraposition, proof by contradiction, and mathematical induction.
3. **Set Theory and Functions**: Set identities, relations, one-to-one and onto functions, inverses, and compositions.
4. **Sequences, Summations, and Recursion**: Recurrence relations, sigma notation, and recursively defined functions.
5. **Algorithmic Analysis**: Pseudocode representation, basic searching and sorting algorithms, and asymptotic orders of growth.
6. **Combinatorics**: Basic counting principles, permutations, and combinations.

## Suggested Progression

To build a robust understanding of the material, the following progression is recommended:
1. Begin with **Logic and Proofs** to establish the formal language and reasoning techniques used throughout the course.
2. Proceed to **Set Theory and Functions** to understand mathematical relations and mappings.
3. Study **Sequences and Induction** to master recursive thinking and inductive proofs.
4. Conclude with **Algorithms and Combinatorics**, bridging the gap between abstract mathematical structures and computational processes.
