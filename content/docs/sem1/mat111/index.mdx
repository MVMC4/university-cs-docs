# MAT111: Introductory Mathematics I

This course serves as a comprehensive pre-calculus foundation, consolidating and building upon secondary school mathematics. It is designed to lead students to a rigorous understanding of advanced algebraic concepts and techniques. The primary objective is to master the algebraic and trigonometric tools required to model, analyze, and solve complex scientific and engineering problems.

## Scope and Coverage

The curriculum focuses heavily on the properties, behaviors, and applications of various mathematical functions. Core topics include:

1. **Functions and Graphs**: Determining domains and ranges, identifying one-to-one mappings, inverse functions, piecewise domains, and graphical transformations (translations and reflections).
2. **Polynomials and Rational Functions**: Polynomial long division, the Remainder Theorem, the Factor Theorem, and the algebra of rational functions.
3. **Exponential and Logarithmic Functions**: Properties, graphical behaviors, and solving complex exponential and logarithmic equations.
4. **Trigonometry**: Derivation of fundamental identities, sine and cosine rules, double and half-angle formulas, and the solution of trigonometric equations.
5. **Complex Numbers**: Argand diagrams, arithmetic operations, de Moivre's theorem, and the extraction of complex roots.

## Syllabus Map

The 2025 course outline and personal class notes organise the module into five connected blocks:

- Functions: domain, range, composition, inverses, graphs, and transformations
- Polynomials and rational functions: division, factors, remainders, and graphs
- Exponential and logarithmic functions and equations
- Trigonometric identities, rules, equations, extrema, and inverse functions
- Complex numbers, Argand diagrams, De Moivre's theorem, and roots

## Suggested Progression

1. Begin with **Functions and Graphs** to establish a strong foundation in mathematical mappings and visual representation.
2. Proceed to **Polynomials, Exponentials, and Logarithms** to master advanced algebraic manipulation and equation solving.
3. Study **Trigonometry** to understand periodic phenomena, geometric relationships, and trigonometric identities.
4. Conclude with **Complex Numbers**, bridging algebra and geometry—a vital prerequisite for advanced engineering, physics, and calculus.
